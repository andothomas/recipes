## Ingredients

### Maple apples (makes 4 portions)

- 4 medium (about 2 lb) | firm apples, such as honeycrisp, gala, or braeburn
- 2 tbsp | unsalted butter
- 3 tbsp | maple syrup
- 1 tsp | ground cinnamon
- 1/4 tsp | ground nutmeg
- 1/4 tsp | fine salt
- 2 tsp | lemon juice
- 1/2 tsp | vanilla extract

### Per bowl

- 1 cup | nonfat plain greek yogurt
- 1 scoop (about 1 oz) | vanilla protein powder
- 2 tbsp | milk or water, to loosen
- 1/4 cup | granola

## Method

### Prep the apples

Peel and core the apples, then dice them into 1/2-inch pieces. Even sizing matters more than exact size — you want every piece tender at the same moment.

> note: I only used 1 apple (honeycrisp).

### Bloom the spices

Melt the butter in a 12-inch skillet over medium heat. Add the cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt and stir for 20 seconds, until the cinnamon smells toasted rather than raw.

> note: use the full 12 inches. Two pounds of apples in a 10-inch pan sit two layers deep and steam into sauce instead of browning.

### Sweat them covered

Add the apples, stir to coat, and cover the pan. Cook 6 minutes, shaking once or twice, until they've released enough juice to pool in the bottom.

> note: there's no added water here. The lid traps the apples' own moisture, which is enough — but it means an uncovered pan will scorch, so keep it on.

![Apples sweating under the lid](photos/apples-covered.jpg)

### Uncover and glaze

Take the lid off, add the maple syrup, and cook 6 to 8 minutes more, stirring every 30 seconds or so. The liquid should reduce to a syrup that coats the back of a spoon and the apple edges should brown.

> note: once the pan liquid is gone the syrup burns in under a minute. Pull it as soon as a paring knife slides through an apple with no resistance.

![Reduced to a glossy syrup](photos/apples-glazed.jpg)

### Finish off heat

Off the burner, stir in the lemon juice and vanilla. Let the pan sit 2 minutes — apples straight off the stove will thin the yogurt to soup.

### Whisk the yogurt base

For each bowl, whisk the protein powder into the yogurt in two additions, adding the milk a splash at a time. Work it until no dry streaks remain, about 45 seconds.

> note: dumping a full scoop into a cup of nonfat greek yogurt at once gives you chalky pockets that never disperse. Two additions and a little liquid is the difference between a base and a paste.

![The base, whisked smooth](photos/yogurt-whisked.jpg)

### Build

Spoon the yogurt base into a bowl. Pile roughly a quarter of the apples in the center, scrape in the syrup clinging to them, and scatter the granola around the edge so it stays crisp.

> note: refrigerate the leftover apples up to 4 days and reheat a portion for 30 seconds. Whisk each yogurt base fresh — protein powder stirred in the night before goes gluey.
